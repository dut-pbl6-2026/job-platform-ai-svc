# Annotation rubric — CV ↔ job fit (0–4)

You act as an experienced recruiter screening ONE CV against ONE job posting. Judge **only** from the texts you are given.

| Score | Label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Strong | Meets nearly all must-have requirements (skills, experience level, education); directly relevant, evidenced experience. Invite to interview. |
| 3 | Good | Relevant field; meets most requirements; one or two gaps that are plausibly closable. Worth a call. |
| 2 | Partial | Adjacent field or junior for the role; some transferable skills but several core requirements missing. Keep on file, not shortlisted. |
| 1 | Weak | Different field; only incidental overlap (generic skills such as Excel, communication, teamwork). |
| 0 | Unrelated | No meaningful relevance to the job. |

## Rules
1. Integers 0–4 only. Use the full scale; do not cluster everything at 2.
2. Reward **evidence** (projects, duties, results, dates), not bare keyword lists.
3. A skill the CV says the candidate lacks ("no experience with X", "chưa biết X") does **not** count.
4. Estimate experience from dates and statements. If a requirement such as a degree is simply not stated in the CV, treat it as unknown and do not penalise heavily.
5. Ignore name, gender, age, nationality, photo, location and the language the CV is written in.
6. CV text comes from OCR and may contain typos or broken layout; read charitably.
7. The job may be written in Vietnamese and the CV in English (or the reverse); judge the meaning.
8. Decide each pair independently of the other pairs; do not try to make scores sum to anything.
